The internal assessment

You design a study to investigate a group of people (the "population of interest") using an experiment, interviews, an observation or a survey, but you do not carry it out. Maximum 2,200 words; the examiner stops reading at the limit. Four criteria, 6 marks each: A Introduction, B Research methodology, C Data collection, D Discussion.

Habits that win marks

  1. Answer the command term. "Describe" wants a detailed account; "explain" adds reasons or causes; "evaluate" weighs strengths and limitations. A description where the question says "evaluate" stays in the low bands.
  2. Paper 1 Section A: explain your example. The top band needs an example that is relevant and explained. A study name on its own is not enough. The guide says details of research studies are not assessed in Section A, so a clear real-life or made-up (hypothetical) example also works; the specimen mark scheme accepts hypothetical examples.
  3. Only your first example counts. The specimen mark schemes credit only the first chemical messenger, behaviour or example you write. Pick one and develop it.
  4. Sections B and C: keep linking. In B, refer back to the situation in the question in every paragraph. In C, tie every point to the named concept and finish with a conclusion that follows from what you argued.
  5. Paper 2 Section A is about your own practical. Answers that describe a method in general, not the practical you did, are capped in the low band (specimen mark scheme). After each class practical write a short note: aim, procedure, sampling technique, findings, ethical points.
  6. Paper 2 Section B and Paper 3 Q4: use the numbers you need. Paper 2 B wants two or more of the listed concepts; Paper 3 Q4 wants at least three sources. Fewer cannot reach the top band.
  7. Learn the vocabulary exactly. Many marks are for "accurate terminology". Use the glossary flashcards: operationalisation, random vs opportunity sampling, internal vs external validity, and so on.
  8. Time. Paper 1 and Paper 2: 35 marks in 90 minutes, about 2.5 minutes a mark, so about 37 minutes for the 15-mark question. Paper 3: 30 marks in 105 minutes, 3.5 minutes a mark.

Command terms

AO1 terms ask for knowledge (shorter answers), AO2 for application and AO3 for evaluation (longer, with arguments on both sides).

Command termAOWhat the IB means (official definition)
DescribeAO1Give a detailed account.
StateAO1Give a specific name, value or other brief answer without explanation or calculation.
AnalyseAO2Break down in order to bring out the essential elements or structure.
ApplyAO2Use an idea, equation, principle, theory or law in relation to a given problem or issue.
CommentAO2Give a judgement based on a given statement or result of a calculation.
DesignAO2Produce a plan, simulation or model.
ExplainAO2Give a detailed account including reasons or causes.
InterpretAO2Use knowledge and understanding to recognize trends and draw conclusions from given information.
PredictAO2Give an expected result.
SuggestAO2Propose a solution, hypothesis or other possible answer.
Compare and contrastAO3Give an account of similarities and differences between two (or more) items or situations, referring to both (all) of them throughout.
DiscussAO3Offer a considered and balanced review that includes a range of arguments, factors or hypotheses. Opinions or conclusions should be presented clearly and supported by appropriate evidence.
EvaluateAO3Make an appraisal by weighing up the strengths and limitations.
ExamineAO3Consider an argument or concept in a way that uncovers the assumptions and interrelationships of the issue.
To what extentAO3Consider the merits or otherwise of an argument or concept. Opinions and conclusions should be presented clearly and supported with appropriate evidence and sound argument.
