Substituting into a formula

A formula is a rule written with letters. To substitute means to replace each letter with its number, then work it out.

Example: un = u1 + (n − 1)d with u1 = 36, n = 13 and d = −6.

  • Write it out with brackets: 36 + (13 − 1) × (−6)
  • Brackets first: 13 − 1 = 12
  • Then multiply: 12 × (−6) = −72
  • Then add: 36 + (−72) = −36

The order is always: brackets, then powers, then × and ÷, then + and −. Put negative numbers in brackets so the minus sign is not lost. The small letter in un is not a power: it just says which position the number is in.

Powers (indices): what 25, (−2)6 and 2−1 mean

The small raised number says how many times to multiply: 25 = 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 = 32, the product of five 2s.

  • A negative number to an even power is positive: (−2)6 = 64. To an odd power it stays negative: (−2)3 = −8.
  • A negative power means 1 divided by: 2−1 = 1/2 and 2−3 = 1/8.

Going backwards: if r3 = 8 then r = 2. If r2 = 3 then r = √3 or r = −√3, because a negative number squared is also positive. On the calculator use the ^ key: 1.03^10 ≈ 1.344.

Solving equations, and a pair of equations together

Solving means finding the value of the letter. Do the same thing to both sides until the letter is alone: 36 + 4d = 12, take 36 from both sides: 4d = −24, divide both sides by 4: d = −6.

Sometimes you have two equations with the same two letters (called simultaneous equations). If both have exactly one u1, subtract one equation from the other and u1 disappears:

u1 + 12d = 8
u1 + 9d = 14
subtract: 3d = −6, so d = −2

Then put d = −2 back into either equation: u1 = 14 − 9 × (−2) = 32. For geometric sequences you divide one equation by the other instead: u1r4 ÷ u1r = r3.

Percentages as multipliers

A percentage is a number out of 100: 3% = 0.03. So 3% of $5000 is 0.03 × 5000 = $150.

To increase or decrease by a percentage in one step, multiply by one number:

increase by 3%100% + 3% = 103%× 1.03
decrease by 15%100% − 15% = 85%× 0.85
decrease by 20%100% − 20% = 80%× 0.8

Doing it again each year means multiplying again: 3 years at 3% is × 1.03 × 1.03 × 1.03 = × 1.033. Two falls of 10% is × 0.92 = × 0.81, a 19% fall, not 20%, because the second 10% is taken from the smaller value. This is how a salary that rises by 3% a year is worked out, and why money that grows by a percentage is a geometric sequence.

Fractions: dividing by a fraction, and adding fractions

The sum to infinity formula often gives a fraction divided by a fraction.

  • Dividing by a fraction is the same as multiplying by it upside down: 12 ÷ 1/3 = 12 × 3 = 36.
  • Take a fraction from 1 by writing 1 as a fraction: 1 − 2/3 = 3/3 − 2/3 = 1/3.
  • To add fractions, give them the same bottom number first: 1/4 + 1/12 = 3/12 + 1/12 = 4/12 = 1/3.

Putting it together: (7/12) ÷ (1 − 7/8) = 7/12 ÷ 1/8 = 7/12 × 8 = 56/12 = 14/3. Paper 1 has no calculator, so practise these by hand.

Rounding: decimal places and significant figures

Decimal places (d.p.) count digits after the decimal point. Significant figures (s.f.) count digits from the first one that is not zero.

5796.3703…2 d.p.5796.37
5796.3703…nearest dollar5796
5796.3703…3 s.f.5800

IB rule: unless the question says otherwise, give answers exactly or correct to 3 significant figures. Money questions usually ask for 2 d.p. or the nearest dollar: read the question and do what it says.

Keep the full calculator value until the very last line. Rounding in the middle of a calculation changes the final answer and can lose the answer mark.

The words and the notation

A sequence is an ordered list of numbers called terms. un is the nth term, so u1 is the first. A series is a sum of terms: Sn is the sum of the first n terms. A sequence is arithmetic when you add the same number each time: the common difference d (it can be negative). Test Learn: u2 − u1 = u3 − u2. Example: 7, 11, 15, 19 has d = 4.

un = u1 + (n − 1)d In booklet
Sn = n2(2u1 + (n − 1)d)   or   Sn = n2(u1 + un) In booklet

From u1 to u5 you take 4 steps, not 5: that is why it is (n − 1)d. The sum works because the series written forwards and backwards pairs up into equal totals:

Worked example 1: two terms given (pack question 3). u1 = 36 and u5 = 12. Find u13, then the n for which Sn = 0.
  1. u5 = u1 + 4d: 36 + 4d = 12, so 4d = −24 and d = −6.
  2. u13 = 36 + 12 × (−6) = −36.
  3. Sn = 0: n2(72 + (n − 1)(−6)) = 0. n ≠ 0, so 78 − 6n = 0 and n = 13.
Why this step? Why 4d in step 1, not 5d?

From the 1st term to the 5th there are 4 steps of size d.

Worked example 2: a term and a sum given (pack question 10). u10 = 14 and S25 = 200. Find k such that uk = 0.
  1. Term: u1 + 9d = 14.
  2. Sum: 252(2u1 + 24d) = 200, so 2u1 + 24d = 16, so u1 + 12d = 8.
  3. Subtract: 3d = −6, so d = −2, and u1 = 14 + 18 = 32.
  4. 32 + (k − 1)(−2) = 0: k − 1 = 16, so k = 17.
Why this step? Why make both equations start with u1 in step 2?

Then subtracting removes u1 and leaves one equation in d alone.

Sigma notation

20Σr = 1(3r + 2) means: put r = 1, 2, …, 20 into 3r + 2 and add. Number of terms Learn: top − bottom + 1.

Worked example 3. Find 20Σr = 1(3r + 2).
  1. 20 − 1 + 1 = 20 terms. First (r = 1): 5. Last (r = 20): 62.
  2. S20 = 202(5 + 62) = 10 × 67 = 670.
Why this step? Why may you use the arithmetic sum formula?

r goes up by 1 each time, so 3r + 2 goes up by 3: the terms are arithmetic with d = 3.

Given Sn, find un

Learn u1 = S1 and un = Sn − Sn−1.

Worked example 4 (pack question 19). Sn = n² + 4n. Find u1, u6 and un.
  1. u1 = S1 = 5. u6 = S6 − S5 = 60 − 45 = 15.
  2. u6 = u1 + 5d: 5d = 10, d = 2, so un = 5 + 2(n − 1) = 2n + 3.
Why this step? Why does S6 − S5 give u6?

The first six terms minus the first five terms leaves only the sixth term.

Real situations

Simple interest pays the same amount each year, so the balance is arithmetic. In pack question 5, 0.315% of $5000 is $15.75 a month, $189 a year: after n years Andy has 5000 + 189n dollars (d = 189).

Worked example 5: data that is nearly arithmetic. A runner's long run in weeks 1 to 5 is 12.1, 13.9, 16.2, 17.8 and 20.0 km. Predict week 10.
  1. The differences (1.8, 2.3, 1.6, 2.2) are close, not equal. Approximate d: (20.0 − 12.1) ÷ 4 = 1.975.
  2. u10 ≈ 12.1 + 9 × 1.975 = 29.875, about 29.9 km.
  3. Comment: week 10 is outside the data, and nobody adds 2 km a week for ever, so the prediction is less reliable the further ahead it goes.
Why this step? Why divide by 4, not 5?

Five values have four gaps between them.

Exam traps. (1) nd instead of (n − 1)d. (2) Term or sum: "in the 13th hour" is u13, "in the first 8 hours" is S8. (3) n is a whole number: "how many complete rows" rounds down, "when does the total first exceed" rounds up. (4) Sigma terms: top − bottom + 1.

SL 1.3

Geometric sequences and series

The pattern: multiply by the same number every time.

By the end of this section I can

A sequence is geometric when you multiply by the same common ratio r each time (r ≠ 0). Test Learn: u2u1 = u3u2. A negative r makes the signs alternate: 3, −6, 12 has r = −2.

un = u1rn−1 In booklet
Sn = u1(rn − 1)r − 1 = u1(1 − rn)1 − r, r ≠ 1 In booklet

Both sum formulas give the same answer: use the first when r > 1, the second when r < 1.

Worked example 1 (pack question 30). Pie slices form a geometric sequence: the 2nd is 30 cm³, the 5th is 240 cm³, the pie is 61 425 cm³. How many slices?
  1. u1r = 30 and u1r4 = 240. Divide: r3 = 8, so r = 2 and u1 = 15.
  2. 15(2n − 1)2 − 1 = 61 425, so 2n = 4096 = 212: 12 slices.
Why this step? Why divide the two equations?

u1 cancels, leaving one equation in r only.

Worked example 2 (pack question 21, calculator). u1 = 50, u4 = 86.4. Find the smallest n with Sn > 33 500.
  1. 50r3 = 86.4, so r3 = 1.728 and r = 1.2. Sn = 250(1.2n − 1).
  2. 250(1.2n − 1) > 33 500 gives 1.2n > 135. GDC table: S26 ≈ 28 369 (too small), S27 ≈ 34 093. n = 27.
Why this step? Why write down S26 and S27?

They show 27 is the first whole number that works; that is often the A1 mark.

Worked example 3 (pack question 19, parts d and e). v2 = 5, v4 = 15 and v99 < 0. Find r and v5.
  1. r2 = 3, so r = ±√3.
  2. v99 = v2r97 with v2 > 0 and 97 odd, so v99 < 0 needs r = −√3. v5 = 15 × (−√3) = −15√3.
Why this step? Why keep both signs in step 1?

(√3)2 and (−√3)2 are both 3; only the extra fact about v99 decides.

Sigma notation and real situations

10Σr = 13(2)r−1 = 3 + 6 + 12 + … has 10 terms, u1 = 3 and r = 2, so it is 3(210 − 1)2 − 1 = 3069. Growth by 3% means multiply by 1.03; a fall of 15% means multiply by 0.85.

SL 1.8 (taught together with geometric sequences)

Sum to infinity

How can infinitely many numbers add up to something finite?

By the end of this section I can

If −1 < r < 1, rn gets closer and closer to 0, so the series has a finite total: it converges. |r| is the modulus of r, its size without the sign; |r| < 1 means −1 < r < 1. If |r| ≥ 1 there is no sum to infinity.

S∞ = u11 − r,   |r| < 1 In booklet
Worked example 1 (pack question 26). Find ∞Σr = 123(78)r.
  1. First term (r = 1): 23 × 78 = 712, not 23.
  2. Ratio 78, and |78| < 1, so the sum exists.
  3. S∞ = 7/121 − 7/8 = 712 × 8 = 143.
Why this step? Why put r = 1 to get the first term?

The sum starts at r = 1, and the power is r, not r − 1, so the first term already has one factor 78.

Worked example 2: the bouncing ball in the banner (pack question 9). A ball is dropped from 10 m; each bounce reaches 23 of the previous height. Find the total distance.
  1. Peaks: 203, 409, …: u1 = 203, r = 23. Their sum: 20/31 − 2/3 = 20 m.
  2. Total: 10 + 2 × 20 = 50 m.
Why this step? Why 10 once but the peaks twice?

The first drop is only down. Every bounce goes up to its peak and then down again.

Finish it. A ball is dropped from 6 m and each bounce reaches 34 of the previous height. Find the total distance.
  1. First peak: 6 × 34 = 4.5 m, and r = 34.

  3. Total distance:

SL 1.4

Financial applications: compound interest and depreciation

Money that grows or shrinks by a percentage is a geometric sequence.

By the end of this section I can

FV = PV × (1 + r100k)kn In booklet

FV future value, PV present value, n years, k compoundings a year (1 yearly, 2 half-yearly, 4 quarterly, 12 monthly), r% the nominal yearly rate.

value after n years = original × (1 − r100)n Learn

Depreciation: the same idea with a decrease.

Worked example 1 (pack question 5(b)). $5000 at 3% compounded yearly for 5 years. Find the value and the interest.
  1. PV = 5000, r = 3, k = 1, n = 5: FV = 5000 × 1.035 = 5796.37…, so $5796. Interest: $796.
Why this step? Why is the multiplier 1.03?

Keeping 100% and adding 3% is 103% of the amount, which is × 1.03 each year.

Worked example 2 (pack question 17). $160 000 at a nominal 5% a year, compounded monthly. Find the value after 6 months.
  1. k = 12 and n = 0.5, so the power is kn = 6.
  2. 160 000 × (1 + 51200)6 = $164 041.89.
  3. GDC financial app: N = 0.5, I% = 5, PV = −160 000, PMT = 0, P/Y = 1, C/Y = 12, solve FV. PV and FV have opposite signs.
Why this step? Why is the power 6, not 0.5?

Interest is added once a month, and 6 months means 6 additions: kn = 12 × 0.5.

Worked example 3 (pack question 15). A car bought for $35 000 loses 15% in year 1, then 11% each later year. Find its value 10 years after purchase.
  1. End of year 1: 35 000 × 0.85 = $29 750.
  2. Nine more years at 11%: 29 750 × 0.899 = 10 423.10…, so $10 423.
Why this step? Why the power 9, not 10?

The first of the ten years was already used, at 15%.

Worked example 4 (pack question 17(d)). $30 000 grows to $41 000 in six years, compounded quarterly. Find r.
  1. 30 000 × (1 + r400)24 = 41 000 (k = 4, kn = 24).
  2. GDC solver or financial app: r = 5.24 (%) to two decimal places.
Why this step? Why 400 under r?

100k with k = 4: the yearly percentage is split into four quarters.

SL 1.6

Simple deductive proof

A proof shows something is true for every case, not just the cases you tried.

By the end of this section I can

= is true for some values: x + 2 = 5 only when x = 3. ≡ (is identical to) is true for every value: (x + 1)2 ≡ x2 + 2x + 1. Most "show that" algebra is an identity Learn.

Layout of an LHS to RHS proof Learn: start with one side only (usually the harder one); change it one justified step at a time (expand, factorise, common denominator); stop when you reach the other side and write "= RHS". Never work on both sides at once: that assumes the answer.

Worked example 1: the guide's own example. Show that (x − 3)2 + 5 ≡ x2 − 6x + 14.
LHS = (x − 3)2 + 5
    = x2 − 6x + 9 + 5expand
    = x2 − 6x + 14 = RHScollect terms
Check, as the guide asks: x = 0 gives 14 on both sides. A check is not a proof, but it catches slips.
Why this step? Why start from the LHS?

It is the side with work to do (a bracket to expand); the RHS is already simplified.

Worked example 2: also from the guide. Show that 1m + 1 + 1m2 + m ≡ 1m. (With m = 3 this is 14 + 112 = 13.)
LHS = mm(m + 1) + 1m(m + 1)factorise, common denominator
    = m + 1m(m + 1) = 1m = RHScancel m + 1
It fails for m = 0 and m = −1, where a denominator is zero.
Why this step? Why factorise m2 + m first?

m(m + 1) shows that m + 1 is already inside it, so it is the common denominator.
