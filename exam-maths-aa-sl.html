Exam technique: Maths AA SL

How the two papers work, the habits that win marks, and what every command word asks for.

The two papers

Paper 1Paper 2
Time1 hour 30 minutes1 hour 30 minutes
CalculatorNone allowedGraphic display calculator (GDC) required, though not every question needs it
Marks80 (40% of the grade)80 (40% of the grade)
LayoutSection A: short questions, about 40 marks. Section B: a few long questions, about 40 marks. All compulsory.Same layout as Paper 1

The other 20% is the internal assessment, the mathematical exploration. Both papers give you a clean copy of the formula booklet. Neither paper is multiple choice.

Habits that win marks

  1. Always show working. The IB guide says full marks are not necessarily awarded for a correct answer with no working. Most marks are for method.
  2. Read the mark scheme letters. M = method mark, A = accuracy (answer) mark, R = reasoning mark, AG = "answer given" (you must reach it, no extra mark for writing it). A mark in brackets, like (M1), can be implied by later correct work.
  3. Accuracy. Unless the question says otherwise, give answers exactly or to 3 significant figures. Keep full calculator values in the working and round only at the end. Money is usually to 2 decimal places or the nearest dollar: read the question.
  4. Paper 1 answers stay exact. Leave fractions, surds and π as they are (√3, 14/3) unless asked to simplify further.
  5. Proper notation, never calculator notation. Write 5.2 × 1030, not 5.2E30 (the guide says calculator notation is not acceptable).
  6. Time. 80 marks in 90 minutes is just over a minute per mark. A 6-mark question should take about 6 to 7 minutes; if you are stuck, move on and come back.
  7. "Show that" lines. When a part says "show that … = 4", the answer is given: the marks are for the steps. If you cannot do it, still use the given result in the next part.
  8. GDC in Paper 2. Know the financial app (TVM: N, I%, PV, PMT, FV, P/Y, C/Y, with PV and FV of opposite sign), the equation solver, tables of values and finding intersections on a graph.

Command terms

Every question starts with one of these words, and it tells you how much to write. The three that cause most lost marks: "Write down" (no working needed, so be quick), "Show that" (every step needed) and "Hence" (you must use the previous part, another method scores nothing).
