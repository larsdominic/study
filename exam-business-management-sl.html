Exam technique: Business Management SL

How the two SL papers work, how the 10-mark answers are marked, and what every command word asks for.

The other 30% is the internal assessment: a business research project on a real business issue, using a conceptual lens, at most 1,800 words (25 marks). Every paper starts with five minutes of reading time. A four-function calculator is enough for every calculation, and graphic calculators are allowed. You get a formula sheet in the exam.

Habits that win marks

  1. Use the case, do not just name it. The markbands punish answers that only mention the company or person's name. Pull specific facts and figures from the case into every point and explain how they support your argument.
  2. Balance and limitations for the top band. A 9 to 10 answer needs arguments for and against, a clear judgement, and a sentence on the limitations of the case or stimulus (missing data, one source, a short time period).
  3. Use the toolkit. SL tools: SWOT, Ansoff matrix, STEEPLE, BCG matrix, business plan, decision trees, descriptive statistics, circular business models. Name the tool, apply it to the case, then draw a conclusion from it.
  4. Define precisely. The guide expects you to define, use and apply business terms correctly. Lead with a precise definition when the command term is "Define", and use the exact term elsewhere.
  5. Prepare the pre-release. Research the context topics in the statement for about five hours, and read the first 200 words of the case carefully: they tell you the business and its situation.
  6. Calculations: show the working ("Calculate" asks for the relevant stages) and give units: %, $, times.
  7. Choose Section B quickly. Read both options for a minute and pick the one where you can bring in a tool and real evidence from the case.
  8. Time. Paper 1 is 30 marks in 90 minutes (3 minutes per mark, so about 30 minutes for the 10-mark question). Paper 2 is 40 marks in 90 minutes (just over 2 minutes per mark).

How the 10-mark questions are marked

Section B extended responses (both papers) are marked with these markbands, best fit. Section A questions use a question-specific markscheme instead.

MarksWhat the answer shows (official markband)
0The work does not reach a standard described by the descriptor.
1 to 2Little understanding of the demands of the question. Little use of business management tools and theories; any tools and theories that are used are irrelevant or used inaccurately. Little or no reference to the stimulus material. No arguments are made.
3 to 4Some understanding of the demands of the question. Some use of business management tools and theories, but these are mostly lacking in accuracy and relevance. Superficial use of information from the stimulus material, often not going beyond the name of the person(s) or name of the organization. Any arguments made are mostly unsubstantiated.
5 to 6The response indicates an understanding of the demands of the question, but these demands are only partially addressed. Some relevant and accurate use of business management tools and theories. Some relevant use of information from the stimulus material that goes beyond the name of the person(s) or name of the organization but does not effectively support the argument. Arguments are substantiated but are mostly one-sided.
7 to 8Mostly addresses the demands of the question. Mostly relevant and accurate use of business management tools and theories. Information from the stimulus material is generally used to support the argument, although there is some lack of clarity or relevance in some places. Arguments are substantiated and have some balance.
9 to 10Clear focus on addressing the demands of the question. Relevant and accurate use of business management tools and theories. Relevant information from the stimulus material is integrated effectively to support the argument. Arguments are substantiated and balanced, with an explanation of the limitations of the case study or stimulus material.

Formulas you are given

These are on the formula sheet in the exam, so learn how to use them, not the formulas themselves.

  • Gross profit margin = gross profit ÷ sales revenue × 100%
  • Profit margin = profit before interest and tax ÷ sales revenue × 100%
  • ROCE = profit before interest and tax ÷ capital employed × 100%, where capital employed = non-current liabilities + equity
  • Current ratio = current assets ÷ current liabilities
  • Acid test (quick) ratio = (current assets − stock) ÷ current liabilities
  • Average rate of return = ((total returns − capital cost) ÷ years of use) ÷ capital cost × 100%

Worked examples for each formula, and the finance and break-even items that are not on the sheet: given vs learn.

Command terms

The level is the assessment objective: AO1 knowledge (short), AO2 application and analysis, AO3 synthesis and evaluation (judgements with evidence), AO4 skills such as calculating and drawing. A question can never use a harder command term than its syllabus topic allows, so "evaluate" only appears where the syllabus says AO3.
