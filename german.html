1. Check your level (5 minutes)

These are the official "I can" statements for level A1 from the Council of Europe's language scale (the CEFR, the European scale of language levels from A1, beginner, to C2, near native), as published by Europass. Read each one and tick it only if it is true for you in German today.

Why tick them: the result below tells you where to start. You had German lessons before, so you may not need to begin at lesson 1; or you may find that the basics need a fresh start. Redo the check every few weeks: it also shows you how far you have come.

Quick test for the speaking line: say out loud now, in German, your name, where you live and who one person in your family is (for example: Ich heiße …, ich wohne in Singapur, das ist meine Mutter, sie heißt …).

2. The goal and the timeline

Goal: a degree taught in German at a German university, starting autumn 2028. For that you need a German certificate at about level C1 (see Certificates). German is not one of your IB subjects, so this runs next to the IB, in small steady steps.

WhenLevel (about)How
Now (Nov 2026)A115 minutes a day during the exam fortnight
End of Mar 2027A1 completeWeekly path: 3 to 4 hours a week, Nicos Weg A1 and its final test
Summer 2027A2Nicos Weg A2 plus a holiday intensive course
End of 2027about B1Nicos Weg B1 (in German only)
Summer 2028about C1, certificateB2 and C1 work, then a full-time course after the IB exams, then the test

Being honest about the time. The Goethe-Institut gives guide figures for the total time to reach each level from zero (Goethe-Institut Glasgow, FAQ): B1 about 260 to 490 hours, C1 about 600 to 750 hours. It says the real time depends on the person, on other languages you already know (you have English and French, which helps) and on whether you live in Germany.

What this plan adds up to (computed, assumptions in brackets):

  • By the end of 2027: about 221 to 276 hours (55 weeks at 3 to 4 hours, plus one 3-week holiday intensive course in summer 2027 of 75 lessons of 45 minutes, about 56 hours). That is around the start of the B1 range (260 hours), so "about B1" is realistic if the habit holds.
  • By the end of April 2028: about 293 to 365 hours (plus 17 weeks at 2 to 3 hours in the IB final year and a 2-week course in the December 2027 holiday, about 38 hours).
  • That leaves about 235 to 457 hours short of the C1 range. A full-time course after the IB exams, June to August 2028 (4 intensive courses of 3 weeks, about 225 hours), brings the total to about 518 to 590 hours: still about 10 to 82 hours below the start of the C1 range. The German you learned before (and the 6 hours of daily sessions now) come on top.

So: C1 by summer 2028 is tight. It is possible if you learn faster than the average (English and French help) and keep the weekly habit every week, with holiday intensives (summer 2027 is the big one) and more hours whenever school allows. Keep a plan B in mind and discuss it with your parents: a German course in Germany that prepares for the university's own test (the DSH) before starting the degree (Goethe University Frankfurt, for example, asks for B1 to join its DSH course, source), or a degree taught in English.

Mind the dates. Many universities want the certificate by the end of the application period, which for an October start is often 15 July (TU Braunschweig, source; also TUM, Frankfurt). A test taken at the end of a June to August 2028 course is too late for those. Routes that fit: a test passed before the deadline, a university that takes your current German proof with the application and lets you pass its own DSH before the semester starts (Viadrina, source), TU Braunschweig's rule for programmes without a selection limit (B2 course at application, certificate at enrolment), or starting a semester later.

4. From 5 Dec: the weekly path

After the exams the time goes up to about 3 to 4 hours a week: 4 sessions of 45 to 60 minutes, one Nicos Weg lesson per session. Rest on 5 and 6 Dec; week 1 starts on Mon 7 Dec.

One session (45 to 60 min):

  1. 5 to 10 min: due German flashcards, then the lesson's Vocabulary tab on DW.
  2. 30 to 40 min: the lesson: video once with subtitles and once without, all exercises, then the Grammar tab (if the lesson has one).
  3. 5 to 10 min: use it. Write five sentences about your own life with the new words and grammar, then read them out loud. Once a week, record yourself for one minute and listen back.

17 weeks, 4 lessons each, from lesson 11 to lesson 76 and the A1 final test (ends Sun 4 Apr 2027). Tick the week when all its sessions are done.

6. Certificates that German universities accept

To study a programme taught in German, you prove your German with a certificate. These are the main ones. Each university, and sometimes each programme, sets its own rule, so always check the admission page of the programme you choose (DAAD).

CertificateResult usually neededWhere you take it
TestDaF (Test Deutsch als Fremdsprache)TDN 4 in all four parts (reading, listening, writing, speaking). This counts for unrestricted admission to all programmes (TestDaF Institute, quoting the national framework rules RO-DT). Some programmes accept less in one part.Test centres in about 100 countries (DAAD); the Goethe-Institut Singapore offers it.
DSH (the universities' own German test)DSH-2 overall counts for all programmes (TUM, Goethe University Frankfurt). Medicine and dentistry at Frankfurt need DSH-3.In Germany, at universities (DAAD).
telc Deutsch C1 HochschulePassed; "usually recognised" (DAAD; accepted at TUM and TU Braunschweig). Some ask for a grade: Frankfurt wants at least "befriedigend" (satisfactory) (source), Viadrina wants "gut" (good) (source).telc exam centres.
Goethe-Zertifikat C2Passed; "usually recognised" (DAAD, TUM, Frankfurt).Goethe-Institut, including Singapore.
Goethe-Zertifikat C1Only "for some degree programmes" (DAAD); Viadrina says C1 is not enough (source). Do not plan on it without checking.Goethe-Institut, including Singapore.
  • Deadline: some universities want the certificate by the end of the application period (TUM, Frankfurt). TU Braunschweig, for programmes without a selection limit, accepts proof of a B2 course at application and the certificate at enrolment (source).
  • uni-assist (the service many universities use to check international applications) lists DSH, TestDaF, Goethe-Institut certificates and the school German diploma DSD as accepted by all universities, with levels that vary (uni-assist).
  • Course attendance papers and DW's course certificate are not proof of German.

7. Your IB subjects and German university entry

Source: the official KMK agreement on recognising the IB Diploma (decision of 10.03.1986 as amended 15.06.2023; Anlage 1 as of 26.03.2026, Anlage 2 as of 19.11.2024), KMK PDF. KMK is the conference of German state education ministers.

  • Your subjects meet the subject rules (rule 1a and 1b): two languages (English A, French B), a science (Biology), Mathematics (Analysis and Approaches), a social science (Psychology, Business Management), and at least one of the Higher Level subjects is a language, maths or a science (English A HL, Biology HL). Every subject needs at least grade 4 (one grade 3 can be balanced by a 5 in another subject at the same or a higher level, with at least 24 points in total: rule 1d).
  • Maths is the catch (footnote 3): with Mathematics AA at Standard Level you only get subject-restricted entry, to degrees outside mathematics, natural sciences and technology. So no maths, science or engineering degree. The exception is schools on the KMK's Anlage 1 or 2 lists; in Singapore only the German European School Singapore is listed. With Mathematics AA at Higher Level you get general entry to all subjects.
  • German proof (rule 1e): German citizens who took the IB at a school abroad without German as a subject must prove enough German before starting a degree in Germany; the federal states set the details. Other applicants need a German certificate anyway (see Certificates). That is what this page is for.

Question for the school: you may want a science or engineering degree. Can you move to Mathematics AA HL, and by when? Without it, the IB alone does not open those degrees (the agreement's rule 2 allows an extra entrance exam, the Feststellungsprüfung, but that costs time).
