What a timed topic test is for

A topic test checks one topic the way an exam does: a fixed time, no notes, marks printed in brackets after each question, for example [3]. Today the times come from the test covers: they are exam pace, a bit faster than normal homework.

The point is not the score. The point is to find out which exact points you cannot yet write down under time, so you can fix them before the exam.

How a mark scheme gives marks

A mark scheme is the list of answers a marker accepts. For a written question it lists separate points, and each different correct point is worth one mark, up to the number in brackets. A scheme often lists more points than there are marks ("any two of these").

  • Say the same thing twice: still one mark.
  • Your words can differ from the scheme if the meaning is the same.
  • A vague word ("sticks" for adhesion) may not get the mark.

Command words: what the question wants

The first word of a question tells you how much to write.

  • State: one word or short phrase.
  • Outline: the main points, briefly.
  • Describe: say what something is like or what happens.
  • Explain: say why or how, with "because" and "so".
  • Compare and contrast: what is the same and what is different.
  • Suggest: a sensible idea, even for something you were not taught.

SL and HL on one paper

Biology is taught at standard level (SL) and higher level (HL). HL students learn everything SL students learn plus some extra parts ("additional higher level").

These InThinking tests are one paper for both levels: the HL-only questions come at the end, and the cover gives two times and two totals. You are HL, so you answer every question and use the HL time and total.

Still stuck on a word? Do not search for long. Write it in the Error log tab of the study sheet starting with "word:" (for example "word: composition") and carry on. It will be explained in the next guide.

  • Q4 [5]: 1 mark per correct label a to e. The scheme accepts any solid line for the covalent bond, except the base letters. Your circle round "a single nucleotide" must hold one phosphate, one sugar and one base.
  • Q5 [2]: any two of its points.   • Q6 [3]: any three of its four points. The heart of it: each separated strand is a template, and A pairs with T, C with G, so the new strand has the same base sequence.
  • Q7 [3]: 1 mark per row: RNA is single stranded; DNA has deoxyribose; RNA has uracil instead of thymine. The scheme underlines single, deoxyribose and uracil: those are the words to use.
  • Q8 [6]: any six of its eight points (base sequence stores the information, huge number of possible sequences, sequence codes for amino acids, small differences within a species, bigger differences between species).
  • Q9 [4], HL: any four of its five points. The scheme says at most 3 marks if your answer does not make clear that DNA, not protein, is the genetic material.
  • Q10 [3], HL: DNA wrapped around histone proteins; eight histones in the core; one more histone with linker DNA joining the nucleosomes.

Exam tips for Biology

Only what these InThinking mark schemes show; they are not official IB rules.

  • One point, one mark. Schemes list more points than marks ("any two of these answers"). For a 3-mark question, write at least three separate, clear points.
  • Use the key word. The schemes underline the words they look for: adhesion, hydrogen bonds, histone, deoxyribose, uracil. A vague word ("sticks", "sugar") may not get the mark.
  • Answer the actual question. A1.2 Q9 caps you at 3 of 4 if you never say that DNA, not protein, is the genetic material.
  • Compare and contrast means both. B1.1 Q6 rewards similarities ("Both ...") and differences, plus named examples.
  • Calculations: show the working. B1.1 Q5 (e) accepts a range (53 to 62%), so a small rounding difference is fine if the method is right.
  • Experiments: name the control and say why. B1.1 Q5 (f) gives 1 mark for the control and 1 for what it shows.
  • Diagrams: exact conventions. A1.1 Q5 wants solid lines for covalent bonds and δ+ and δ−, not full charges.
