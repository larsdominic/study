English Paper 1 style analysis, one cartoon
67:00
Ready.

Your revision guide (step 7) gives HL Paper 1 as 2 hours 15 minutes for two texts, and suggests per text: read and annotate 10 min, plan 10 min, write 40 min, check 7 min. That is 67 minutes.

The task67 min
  1. Read and annotate (10 min). Read the guiding question first. Note the audience, the purpose and the target of the satire. Annotate the cartoon with the Big 5 (audience, purpose and context; content and theme; tone, mood and register; style; structure and layout) from your Big 5 slides, and the conventions from steps 2 to 4 of the revision guide: caricature, exaggeration, symbolism, juxtaposition, scale and placement, caption, labels, irony, colour.
  2. Plan (10 min). Write a one-sentence thesis that answers the guiding question. Choose 3 or 4 points and group them by argument, not in the order you noticed them. The Horsey model groups them as: the contrast between the figures; the central object and setting; the language and tone; words and image working together.
  3. Write (40 min). Introduction: text type, cartoonist, publication, date, audience, purpose, then your thesis. Each body paragraph in PEEL, using the step 5 chain from the revision guide: spot it, explain it, the effect on the reader, the link to the purpose. Never stop at naming a technique. Conclusion: judge how well the cartoon works, not only what it means. The Horsey notes say 600 to 800 focused words can reach the top bands.
  4. Check (7 min). Fix errors, sharpen your terms, and check that every topic sentence goes back to the guiding question.
Self-checkabout 7 min
  1. Fill in the "Check yourself against the criteria" table at the end of the revision guide (step 7): for A understanding and interpretation, B analysis and evaluation, C focus and organisation, D language, tick Not yet, Nearly or Secure.
  2. Compare with "What lifts this response to the top band" in the Horsey file. Tick each one you did: analysis, not description (every observation followed by "which suggests" or "so that"); words and image read together; one or two points most students would miss; a conclusion that evaluates.
  3. Underline your thesis and each topic sentence: do they all answer the guiding question (C)? Circle the subject terms you used (D) and compare with the Horsey ones (juxtaposition, lexis, semantic field, bathos, passive voice).
  4. Give yourself a rough mark out of 5 for each criterion, using the Horsey 5 out of 5 notes as the top. Enter the total out of 20 below, then hand the analysis to your English teacher and replace your mark with theirs when you get it.
  5. Write the weakest criterion in the Error log, with one thing you will do differently next time.

Where these figures come from: the 2 hours 15 minutes, 20 marks per text, 5 marks per criterion and the criteria A to D are all from your class revision guide (step 7) and the Horsey model. The official IB Language and Literature guide is not in your Drive, so check them with your teacher.

My points for the evaluating research task, Q5 and Q6 (not the teacher's mark scheme)

Q5 [4]: one mark for each point that is explained and tied to Ms Patel's study.

  • Reliability: she wrote the 5-item questionnaire herself and never tested it, for example by giving it twice to the same students to see if the scores stay the same (test-retest). So we do not know if it gives consistent scores.
  • Construct validity: we do not know whether the items measure exam anxiety, the construct, rather than general stress or mood; it was never compared with an established, already tested anxiety measure.
  • The students knew the aim, so their answers may show what they think she wants to hear, which also weakens what the scores mean.
  • Improvement: use an established anxiety scale, or pilot her questionnaire, check test-retest reliability and compare its scores with an established scale.

Q6 [4]: about 2 marks for population validity and 2 for ecological validity, each 1 for the problem with evidence from the study and 1 for what it means for her claim.

  • Population validity: 24 volunteers (self-selected through a library poster) from one Grade 11 class in one school, all her own students. Volunteers may be more interested in breathing exercises or more anxious, so the sample does not represent "all IB students".
  • Ecological validity: a mock exam at school is quite close to real life, which is a strength, but a mock has lower stakes than a real IB exam, and the exercise was led by the students' own teacher, so the effect may be different in real exams.
  • Judgement: the claim is far too broad. At most the study suggests the exercise might help these students in this setting, and the word "cause" is not justified while order effects and her own marking are not controlled.
Numbers from specimen source 2, worked out to check your Q2 analysis (open after the timer)
  • 25 students in each group; scores out of 30, and higher means better mental health.
  • Mean: 11.6 with high screen time against 17.64 with no screen time, so 6.04 points lower. Median: 10 against 15, so 5 lower.
  • Standard deviation: 3.87 against 4.48, so the no screen time scores are a little more spread out.
  • Range: 21 − 7 = 14 and 25 − 12 = 13, about the same.
  • Box plot: the middle half of the high screen time group runs from 9 to 14 and of the no screen time group from 14 to 21, so the two boxes only meet at 14.

The markscheme decides the marks. A strong conclusion also says what the data cannot show: one day and one task only.
