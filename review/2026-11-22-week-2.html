Psychology Paper 1 Section A style, 4 marks

Describe the multi-store model of memory with reference to one example. [4]

Use a different example from the 16 Nov worked example (Peterson and Peterson): try the serial position effect.

Show model answer (only after you have written yours on paper)

Model answer (written for this site, not an official IB answer):

Point. The multi-store model (Atkinson and Shiffrin, 1968) describes memory as three separate stores: sensory memory, short-term memory (STM) and long-term memory (LTM). Information flows one way: attention moves it from sensory memory into STM, which holds about 7 ± 2 items for about 15 to 30 seconds without rehearsal, and rehearsal moves it into LTM, which has no known limit.

Evidence. The serial position effect (Glanzer and Cunitz, 1966): when people hear a list of words and recall it straight away, they remember the first words and the last words better than the words in the middle.

Explain. The first words had the most time to be rehearsed, so they were passed on to LTM. The last words are still in STM at the moment of recall. The middle words were rehearsed little and were pushed out of STM by newer words (displacement). Two separate effects fit two separate stores, which is what the model describes.

Link. So the model explains why forgetting depends on whether information was rehearsed into LTM.

Check it against the official markband (Paper 1 Section A, IB Psychology guide p45):

  • 1 to 2 marks: "The response demonstrates limited knowledge relevant to the question." "The example is relevant but is not explained."
  • 3 to 4 marks: "The response demonstrates detailed knowledge relevant to the question." "The example is relevant and explained."

Tick list: three stores named? attention and rehearsal as the routes between them? at least one capacity or duration? one example (only the first counts)? each part of the example matched to a part of the model (first words: LTM; last words: STM)? a link sentence, and no evaluation? All six: 3 to 4 band. Example named but not explained: 1 to 2. See the 16 Nov memory section.

Business Paper 1 style, short case, 4 marks

PetPal sells pet food and accessories from 3 shops. Its 15 sales staff earn a fixed salary. Sales have not grown for a year, so the owner plans to pay a lower basic salary plus 5% commission on what each person sells.

Explain one advantage and one disadvantage for PetPal of paying its sales staff commission. [4]

Show model answer (only after you have written yours on paper)

Model answer (written for this site, not an official IB answer):

Advantage. Commission links pay to sales, so each of the 15 staff now has a reason to sell more, for example suggesting a lead or a bed to a customer who came in for food. This could end PetPal's year of flat sales, and PetPal's pay bill only rises when sales rise.

Disadvantage. Pay now falls when sales fall. In quiet months staff earn less and may feel insecure (security is a hygiene factor in Herzberg's theory), so some may leave and PetPal must recruit and train new staff. Staff in the same shop may also compete for customers, which can harm teamwork.

How to mark it (the 2 + 2 pattern from your 19 Nov Business guide: each a point plus why, tied to the case; not an official mark scheme): advantage identified (1) and explained with PetPal's case (1); disadvantage identified (1) and explained with PetPal's case (1). No case facts (15 staff, 3 shops, flat sales, pet products): only the first mark of each pair. Describing piece rate or a pay rise instead of commission: 0 for that half.

Psychology · Mon 16 Nov1. Amira is nervous about her driving test. Her cousin, who is the same age and was just as nervous, passed last month. Now Amira believes she can pass too. Which source of self-efficacy raised her belief?

Seeing someone like you succeed is a vicarious experience. Mastery experiences are her own past successes; social persuasion is being told she can do it; emotional and physical states are her mood and body. Do not mix it up with vicarious reinforcement, which is watching a model being rewarded. Revisit: 16 Nov, social learning theory

Maths · Tue 17 Nov2. The largest possible domain of f(x) = 1x + 4 is

You cannot divide by zero, and x + 4 = 0 when x = −4, so x ≠ −4. Every other number, above or below −4, is allowed. x ≠ 4 has the wrong sign; x > −4 and x ≥ −4 throw away all the numbers below −4 (that is the kind of rule a square root gives, not a fraction). Revisit: 17 Nov, SL 2.2 domain and range

Business · Thu 19 Nov3. A firm brings in new stock-control software. Several older warehouse staff say they are worried they will not cope with the computers. Which strategy tackles this cause most directly?

The cause is fear of not coping (low tolerance of change), so training and time to adjust remove it, although that costs money. Coercion forces the change through and may make staff leave; manipulation risks trust; negotiation (for example a bonus) fits a group that will clearly lose something, not a worry about skills. Revisit: 19 Nov, 2.1 resistance to change

English · Fri 20 Nov4. A panel shows a tiny figure in a huge desert, seen from high above. Which shot type and angle?

Tiny in a huge setting is an extreme long shot; looking down from above is a high angle. Shot type (how much we see) and angle (where we look from) are two separate choices. Together they usually make the figure look isolated and vulnerable. Revisit: 20 Nov, shots and angles

Biology · Wed 11 Nov (week 1)5. In a sample of double-stranded DNA, 22% of the bases are thymine. What percentage of the bases are cytosine?

T pairs with A, so A is also 22%, and A + T = 44%. The remaining 56% is shared equally by G and C: 56 ÷ 2 = 28% cytosine. 56% forgets to halve; 22% assumes all four bases are equal; 78% is 100 − 22. Revisit: 11 Nov, A1.2 base pairing

Psychology · Mon 16 Nov6. Remembering the phone number 0 7 9 1 2 0 3 4 5 6 as 079, 120, 3456 makes it easier to hold in short-term memory. This is

Grouping items into bigger units is chunking: 10 digits become 3 chunks, so short-term memory (7 ± 2 items, or about 4 chunks) can hold them. Maintenance rehearsal is repeating; displacement is new items pushing old ones out; retrieval is getting a memory back. Revisit: 16 Nov, memory models

Maths · Tue 17 Nov7. f(x) = 2x − 1 and g(x) = x2. The value of (g ∘ f)(3) is

In (g ∘ f)(3), f acts first: f(3) = 5, then g(5) = 25. 17 is (f ∘ g)(3) = f(9), the wrong order; 14 adds f(3) + g(3); 45 multiplies them. A composite is neither a sum nor a product. Revisit: 17 Nov, SL 2.5 composite functions

Biology · Wed 18 Nov8. Catalase releases 30 cm3 of oxygen in 75 s. The rate of reaction is

Rate = amount ÷ time = 30 ÷ 75 = 0.4, and the unit is amount per time: cm3 s−1. 2.5 divides the time by the amount; 2 250 multiplies; "0.4 cm3" has lost the per-second part of the unit, which often has its own mark. Revisit: 18 Nov, C1.1.9 measuring rates

Business · Thu 12 Nov (week 1)9. A coffee brand lets local owners open cafés under its name. Each owner pays a fee plus a share of sales. This method of growth is

A joint venture sets up a new company owned by both firms; a merger combines two firms into one; a takeover gains control of another firm, even against its managers' wishes. Psychology · Sat 14 Nov (week 1)10. A reaction-time app always adds exactly 50 ms to every time it records. The app is

It makes the same error every time, so its results are consistent (reliable) but always wrong (not valid), like a bathroom scale that is always 2 kg too heavy. A valid measure must also be reliable, so "valid but not reliable" cannot happen. Revisit: 14 Nov, reliability and validity

Business · Thu 19 Nov11. A retailer has a chief executive, 4 directors, 16 area managers and 64 store managers. It removes all the area managers, so the store managers report straight to the directors. What is each director's span of control now?

The 64 store managers are shared between 4 directors: 64 ÷ 4 = 16. Span of control counts only direct reports. 4 was each director's span before (16 ÷ 4); 64 is all the store managers; 20 adds the old and new numbers. Wider spans and fewer levels are what delayering does. Revisit: 19 Nov, 2.2 structure

English · Fri 20 Nov13. A panel shows a smiling family on a beach. The caption reads: "A week later, the war began." Which word-picture combination is this?

Biology · Wed 18 Nov14. A protein has two polypeptide chains, of 40 and 25 amino acids, joined to each other by disulfide bonds. How many peptide bonds does it contain?

Maths · Tue 17 Nov17. The range of f(x) = 3 − x2, x ∈ ℝ, is

x2 is never negative, so 3 − x2 is at most 3 (at x = 0) and goes down without limit: f(x) ≤ 3. "x ≤ 3" writes the range with x, which the mark scheme does not accept; f(x) ≥ 3 has the direction wrong; f(x) ≥ 0 is the range of a square root. Revisit: 17 Nov, SL 2.2 range

Biology · Sun 15 Nov (week 1)18. A triglyceride forms from one glycerol and three fatty acids. Which bonds form, and what happens to water?

Each fatty acid joins glycerol by a condensation reaction that makes one ester bond and releases one water molecule: three of each. Glycosidic bonds join sugars; using water up is hydrolysis, the reverse. Revisit: 15 Nov, B1.1.9 triglycerides

Business · Thu 19 Nov19. A research lab's manager sets the goal and the deadline, then lets her team of experienced scientists decide how to reach it. Her leadership style is

Setting the goal and letting expert, self-motivated staff decide how is laissez-faire. It is not "no leader": she still sets the goal. Autocratic and paternalistic leaders decide alone; situational means switching between styles to fit the task. Revisit: 19 Nov, 2.3 leadership

Psychology · Mon 16 Nov20. Reaction times (ms) of six students: 300, 320, 340, 360, 380, 1 000. The median is

Six values, already in order, so the median is the mean of the 3rd and 4th: (340 + 360) ÷ 2 = 350 ms. 340 or 360 takes only one of the middle two; 450 ms is the mean (2 700 ÷ 6), pulled up by the outlier of 1 000 ms, which is why the median is the better average here. Revisit: 16 Nov, HL averages

Biology · Wed 18 Nov23. Which of the bonds that hold a protein's tertiary structure is covalent?

The disulfide bond (−S−S−) between the sulfur atoms of two cysteines is covalent, the strongest of the four. The other three are weaker, which is why heat and pH can break them and denature the protein. Revisit: 18 Nov, B1.2.9 tertiary structure

Maths · Tue 17 Nov24. f(x) = 4x − 7. f−1(x) is

Psychology · Mon 9 Nov (week 1)25. A study compares revising with flashcards and rereading. The flashcard group revises in a quiet library, the rereading group in a noisy canteen. The flashcard group scores higher. The noise is

The noise was not controlled and differs between the conditions, so it could explain the result instead of the revision method: a confounding variable. English · Fri 20 Nov29. Panel 1: a girl packs a suitcase in her bedroom at night. Panel 2: the same girl, years older, walks out of an airport in another country. Which panel transition is this?

A jump across a lot of time and space is scene-to-scene: the reader fills a big gap (closure). Action-to-action shows one subject doing one thing after another; moment-to-moment shows almost no change; aspect-to-aspect shows different parts of one place or mood. Revisit: 20 Nov, panels and closure

With a non-competitive or mechanism-based inhibitor, or a denatured enzyme, more substrate does not help. Revisit: 18 Nov, C1.1 inhibitors (HL)
