Psychology Paper 1 Section A style, 4 marks

Describe operant conditioning with reference to one example. [4]

Show model answer (only after you have written yours on paper)

Model answer (written for this site, not an official IB answer):

Operant conditioning is learning through the consequences of a behaviour: a behaviour followed by reinforcement becomes more likely, and a behaviour followed by punishment becomes less likely. Positive reinforcement adds something pleasant, such as a reward; negative reinforcement takes away something unpleasant; both make the behaviour more frequent. Punishment adds something unpleasant or takes away something pleasant, so the behaviour becomes less frequent. For example, a teacher gives a disruptive student a merit point every lesson he stays in his seat. Because staying seated is now followed by a reward he values (positive reinforcement), he links the behaviour with its consequence and stays in his seat more often.

Check it against the official markband (Paper 1 Section A, IB Psychology guide p45):

  • 1 to 2 marks: "limited knowledge"; the example "is relevant but is not explained".
  • 3 to 4 marks: "detailed knowledge"; the example "is relevant and explained".

Tick list: learning through consequences? reinforcement increases, punishment decreases? positive and negative used correctly ("negative" = taken away)? one example (only the first counts)? the example says how the consequence changes the behaviour? All five: 4. Example named but not explained: 2 at most.

Business Paper 1 style, short case, 4 marks

FreshBowl sells salads from 5 outlets in one city. The owners plan to open 15 more over the next two years, so the chain will have 20 outlets.

Explain two internal economies of scale FreshBowl could gain from this growth. [4]

Show model answer (only after you have written yours on paper)

Model answer (written for this site, not an official IB answer):

Purchasing economies. With 20 outlets FreshBowl buys far more vegetables, bowls and packaging, so suppliers give bulk discounts. Each salad then costs less to make: its average cost falls.

Marketing economies. One advertising campaign or one ordering app can now be spread over the sales of 20 outlets instead of 5, so the marketing cost of each salad sold falls.

How to mark it (the 1 + 1 pattern from your Business guide's worked examples; not an official mark scheme): for each economy, 1 mark for naming a correct type, 1 mark for explaining it with FreshBowl's case (outlets, salads, ingredients) and saying it lowers average cost. Financial (cheaper loans for a bigger firm) or managerial (specialist managers) economies, explained with the case, also score. Writing "costs go down" without "per salad" or "average" loses the second mark.

Biology short written answer, 3 marks

Explain why glucose dissolves in water but olive oil does not. [3]

Show model answer (only after you have written yours on paper)

Mark points (written for this site in the style of your class mark schemes, one mark per point, max 3; not an official IB mark scheme, and the IB Biology guide is not in your folder):

  • water molecules are polar (δ− oxygen, δ+ hydrogens);
  • glucose is polar / has many -OH groups, so it forms hydrogen bonds with water / is hydrophilic;
  • water molecules surround each glucose molecule, so it dissolves;
  • oil is non-polar / hydrophobic, so it is not attracted to water and cannot form hydrogen bonds with it (it stays as a separate layer);

See A1.1 "Water as a solvent" in your Biology guide if you missed one.

Mixed quiz: week 1

The subject appears after you answer. First decide: which subject, which method?

Biology1. Sweating cools the body mainly because water has a high

Evaporating sweat must break hydrogen bonds, which takes a lot of heat from the skin: that is the latent heat of vaporisation. Specific heat capacity is about warming water up, not evaporating it.

Maths2. An arithmetic sequence has u1 = 5 and d = −3. Find u10.

u10 = 5 + 9 × (−3) = −22. −25 uses 10 steps instead of 9; 32 adds instead of subtracting.

Business3. A firm sells a product for $50. Bought-in materials and services cost $30. Its added value is

Added value = selling price − cost of bought-in materials and services = 50 − 30 = $20. It is not profit: wages, rent and profit all come out of it.

Maths4. Find the sum of the first 10 terms of 2 + 5 + 8 + …

S10 = 10/2 (2 × 2 + 9 × 3) = 5 × 31 = 155. 140 and 170 use 8 or 10 differences instead of 9; 310 forgets to halve.

Biology5. In DNA, guanine pairs with

G pairs with C by three hydrogen bonds; A pairs with T by two. A purine always pairs with a pyrimidine.

Maths6. The 6th term of the geometric sequence 81, 27, 9, … is

r = 13, u6 = 81 × (13)5 = 81243 = 13. 19 uses the power 6 instead of 5.

Psychology7. In your class Skittles experiment, a buzzer sounded just before each Skittle was eaten. After several pairings, students salivated when they heard the buzzer alone. Salivating at the buzzer alone is the

It is a learned response to the buzzer, so it is the conditioned response (CR). The buzzer is now the conditioned stimulus (CS); before the pairings it was a neutral stimulus. Salivating at the Skittle itself is the unconditioned response (UCR): nobody had to learn it.

Business8. Which of these is a tertiary sector business?

Tertiary = services to people (shops, banks). A mine is primary, a factory secondary, and research and software are quaternary (knowledge).

Maths9. The sum to infinity of 81 + 27 + 9 + … is

r = 13, so S∞ = 811 − 1/3 = 81 × 32 = 121.5. 243 divides by r instead of 1 − r.

Biology10. Which statement about RNA is correct?

RNA: one strand, ribose, uracil instead of thymine. DNA: two strands, deoxyribose, thymine.

Maths11. Σ from r = 1 to 5 of 2r equals

The terms are 2, 4, 8, 16, 32 (start at r = 1, so the first term is 2), total 62. 31 is 1 + 2 + 4 + 8 + 16, starting from 20.

Business12. Two firms set up a new, separate company that they own together. This is

A joint venture creates a new business owned jointly. In a strategic alliance the firms cooperate but no new business is created; a merger combines the two firms into one.

Maths13. The geometric series with first term 1 and ratio x3 has a sum to infinity when

The condition is |r| < 1, so |x/3| < 1, which is |x| < 3, that is −3 < x < 3. "x < 3" forgets the negative side.

Biology14. How many different base sequences are possible for a stretch of 3 DNA bases?

4 choices at each position: 4 × 4 × 4 = 43 = 64. 12 multiplies 4 × 3; 81 is 34, the wrong way round. This is why codons are triplets: 64 codes are enough for 20 amino acids.

Maths15. $5000 is invested at 4% compounded annually. Its value after 2 years is

5000 × 1.042 = 5000 × 1.0816 = $5408. $5400 is simple interest (4% of the original twice).

Psychology16. A student's parents stop nagging her about homework as soon as she has finished it, and she starts doing her homework earlier. In operant conditioning this is

Something unpleasant (the nagging) is taken away when she does the behaviour, and the behaviour increases: negative reinforcement. Reinforcement always makes a behaviour more likely, punishment less likely. "Negative" means something is taken away, not that it is bad.

Business17. Which of these stakeholders is internal?

Internal stakeholders are inside the organisation: employees, managers and directors, and shareholders. The others are external.

Maths18. A car worth $30 000 loses 20% of its value each year. Its value after 2 years is

30 000 × 0.82 = 30 000 × 0.64 = $19 200. $18 000 takes 20% of the original price twice.

Biology19. Water climbs up a thin glass tube and forms a meniscus mainly because of

Adhesion: water molecules form hydrogen bonds with the polar glass surface, and cohesion drags the molecules behind them up too. This is capillary action, also seen in cell walls and soil.

Maths20. Which symbol shows that a statement is true for every value of x?

≡ means "is identical to" (an identity). = holds only for particular values.

Business21. A sole trader closes with debts larger than the business assets. Who must pay the rest?

A sole trader has unlimited liability: the owner is personally liable for all business debts. Limited liability (losing at most what you invested) applies to shareholders of companies.

Maths22. Which completes a valid proof? "For any integer n, n + (n + 1) = 2n + 1, so the sum of two consecutive integers is …"

2n is always even, so 2n + 1 is always odd. An example (3 + 4 = 7) is not a proof.

Psychology23. A study tests whether music helps memory: one randomly allocated group revises with music, the other in silence, then both sit the same test. What is the independent variable?

The IV is what the researcher changes (here two conditions: music or silence). The test score is what is measured, so it is the dependent variable.

Psychology24. In a repeated measures experiment, half the participants do condition A first and then B; the other half do B first and then A. This is done to control

This is counterbalancing. In repeated measures the same people do both conditions, so participant variables are already controlled. The risk is that doing one condition first (practice, boredom, tiredness) changes the scores in the second; counterbalancing spreads these order effects evenly over both conditions.

Psychology25. Which of these is a quasi-experiment?

Age groups already exist, so participants cannot be randomly allocated to them: that makes it a quasi-experiment, which cannot establish cause and effect. The other three use random allocation (a coin toss counts).

Psychology26. A researcher puts every student's name in a hat and draws 30 names. This is

Every member of the population has an equal chance of being picked: random sampling. Random allocation is something else: deciding by chance which condition each chosen participant goes into.

Psychology27. At HL, Paper 3 (data analysis) is worth what share of the final grade?

HL: Paper 1 25%, Paper 2 25%, Paper 3 30%, internal assessment 20%. 35% is Paper 1 or Paper 2 at SL.
